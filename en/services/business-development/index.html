Business plan development

1

Market analysis and marketing strategy

  • Market size and segmentation: Identification of the target audience and market share forecasts.
  • Competitive analysis: Comparative assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of key competitors.
  • Marketing mix (4P): Development of product, pricing, distribution channels, and promotion strategy.
2

Operational and production plan

  • Logistics and supply chain: The process from raw material procurement to final product delivery.
  • Technical specifications: Requirements for equipment, IT infrastructure, and facilities.
  • Human resources: Building the organizational structure, staffing schedule, and payroll planning.
3

Financial modelling

  • Capital expenditures (CAPEX): Initial funds required to launch the project.
  • Operating expenditures (OPEX): Monthly and annual fixed and variable costs.
  • Financial statements: Profit & Loss (P&L), Cash Flow, and Balance Sheet forecasts (3–5 years).
  • Profitability indicators: Payback Period, NPV (Net Present Value), and IRR (Internal Rate of Return).

Feasibility analysis of new business ideas

1

Market fit audit

  • Problem validation: Does the problem the idea addresses truly exist, and are people willing to pay for its solution?
  • Target audience analysis: Is there a sufficiently large customer base for the idea (TAM, SAM, SOM analysis)?
  • Value proposition: What differentiates your offering from competitors? The "Why you?" question is answered.
2

Technical & operational audit

  • Execution capability: Does the team have the necessary knowledge and experience to bring this idea to life?
  • Resource availability: How accessible are the required technology, equipment and raw materials?
  • Scalability: As the business grows, will costs and quality remain manageable?
3

Financial sustainability audit

  • Profit margin: Is the difference between the selling price and cost of goods sufficient for the business to survive?
  • Break-even point: From what point will the business begin to cover its costs and transition to net profit?
  • Investment requirement: What is the realistic budget needed for the project launch and the "valley of death" (the period of negative cash flow)?
4

Risk & governance

  • Risk matrix: Grouping of probable risks (legal, financial, reputational) by impact level using color codes (Red/Yellow/Green).
  • Compliance: Assessment of the idea's conformity with local and international standards (e.g. GDPR, anti-corruption, tax legislation).

Investment attraction & matching services

1

Pre-deal preparation

  • Pitch deck preparation: A document presenting the essence of the project, the market problem and its solution in 10–15 slides in a visual and compelling manner.
  • Financial model development: 3–5 year revenue and expense projections, return on investment (ROI) and project valuation.
  • Use of Funds plan: Justification of how the raised capital will be allocated by percentage (e.g. 40% marketing, 30% R&D, 30% operations).
2

Investor relations & strategy

  • Investor pipeline development: Preparation of a list of potential investors aligned with the project's industry and stage (Seed, Series A, etc.).
  • Teaser preparation: Sending one-page executive summaries that highlight the project's attractiveness without disclosing the company name.
  • Negotiation management: Organizing meetings with investors and providing support during pitching sessions.
3

Due diligence support

  • Data room setup: Collecting all legal, financial, operational and tax documents of the company in a digital and organized folder.
  • Risk audit: Identifying and resolving potential issues in advance (e.g. legal gaps or non-transparent expenses) that may surface during the review process.
4

Deal closing

  • Term sheet analysis: Auditing the favorability of the investor's proposed terms (equity split, governance participation rights, liquidation preference, etc.) for company founders.
  • Shareholders agreement (SHA): Advisory support in the legal documentation of new partnership terms.

Services in strategic management

1

Strategic planning and corporate strategy

  • Market diagnostics: Identifying threats and opportunities in the macro and micro environment through PESTEL and Porter's five forces analysis.
  • Unified vision formation: Defining the company's mission, vision and strategic pillars.
  • Portfolio strategy: Analytical recommendations for holdings on which business lines to retain and which to sell (divestiture).
2

Target operating model (TOM)

  • Structure optimisation: Building the most agile organisational structure (e.g. matrix or functional) capable of executing the strategy.
  • Process design: Rebuilding core business processes (value chain) in line with international standards.
  • Governance accountability: Clear distribution of decision-making authorities (RACI matrix).
3

Results-based management and KPI systems

  • Balanced scorecard (BSC): Balancing the strategy across 4 perspectives (financial, customer, internal processes, learning and development).
  • Cascading (vertical integration): Breaking down company objectives to department and individual employee level.
  • Performance management: Linking KPIs to the bonus and motivation system.
4

Digital strategy and transformation

  • IT-business integration: Auditing whether the technological infrastructure serves the company's business objectives.
  • Data strategy: A roadmap for converting the data collected by the company into a strategic advantage (business intelligence).
  • Automation: Strategic implementation of routine process automation (RPA) and artificial intelligence solutions.

General business audit and report preparation

1

Financial health audit (financial audit)

  • Liquidity and profitability: Assessing the company's ability to meet short-term obligations and comparing net profit margin against the industry average.
  • Cash flow: Checking for cash gap risk even when the business appears profitable "on paper".
  • Cost structure: Ratio of fixed to variable costs and identification of "dead" costs (unused resources).
2

Operational and process audit (operational audit)

  • Business process mapping: Identifying redundancy and bottlenecks within processes.
  • Target operating model (TOM): Evaluating whether the existing management model serves the strategic objectives.
  • Supply chain and logistics: Vendor audit and procurement effectiveness.
3

Strategic and market position audit (commercial audit)

  • Market share and customer loyalty: Customer churn rate and cost of acquiring a new customer (CAC).
  • VRIO analysis: Assessing the extent to which the company's resources are rare, valuable and inimitable.
  • Competitor benchmarking: Technological and service gaps compared to competitors.
4

Human resources and corporate governance (HR & governance)

  • Organisational structure: Evaluating the optimality of management layers (is there excessive bureaucracy?).
  • KPI and motivation system: Synchronisation of employee goals with company objectives.
  • Talent management: Succession planning coverage for critical positions.
5

Technological and digital audit (IT audit)

  • Infrastructure compatibility: Whether existing IT systems can keep pace with the company's growth rate.
  • Data security: Level of cyber risks and protection of customer data.
  • Digitalisation level: Identifying which processes are still performed manually and are causing losses.
